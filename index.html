Priority Word Meanings in Elementary Education

Explore the Words Worth Teaching (WWT) database classifications. This table is filtered to show only verbally-based (V1 & V2) meanings, which are especially relevant for direct classroom instruction. This work is based on Biemiller, A. (2010). Words worth teaching: Closing the vocabulary gap. Columbus, OH: SRA/McGraw-Hill.

About this page: This webpage presents a refined set of word-meaning pairs from Biemiller’s Words Worth Teaching database. The study used AI to classify WWT meanings according to a developmental P/V framework grounded in developmental cognitive science, including work on conceptual development and children’s developing conceptual structures [1].

The purpose of this page is to help teachers, researchers, and curriculum designers explore WWT meanings that may be especially worth considering for direct vocabulary instruction. The database below is filtered to show V1 and V2 meanings. These are not simply “hard words.” They are meanings whose acquisition depends on language, convention, symbolic systems, institutions, formal categories, or rule-governed reasoning [1].

Verbally-based meanings shown on this page:
  • V1: Verbally or institutionally defined entities, concepts, states, units, roles, and categories. This category is used for meanings that cannot be understood through direct perception alone and depend on language, social convention, law, measurement, institutions, symbolic categories, or abstract conceptual systems.
  • V2: Symbolic, linguistic, institutional, formal, or rule-governed procedures and actions. This category is used for meanings referring to actions or procedures that require language, symbols, formal rules, calculation, institutional conventions, or culturally taught procedures.
This refined pool is intended as a practical starting point. It does not represent all vocabulary children need to learn. Rather, it identifies a theoretically motivated subset of WWT word meanings that may be more likely to require explicit teaching than meanings children can often learn through direct experience, demonstration, or incidental exposure [1].
WWT priority key:
  • E = Easyn.
  • T2 = Teach in the primary grades.
  • L2 = Low priority in the primary grades.
  • T6 = Teach in the upper elementary grades.
  • L6 = Low priority in upper elementary grades.
  • D = Difficult: may be more appropriate for Grades 7+.
